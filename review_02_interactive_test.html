📖 Phần 4: Academic Reading Passage (~560 words)

Urban Metamorphosis: Balancing Historic Preservation, Modern Infrastructure, and Community Living

[Paragraph A] Over the past century, urban centers worldwide have undergone unprecedented spatial metamorphosis. The traditional social fabric, once anchored by cohesive extended families residing in low-rise residential quarters, has rapidly yielded to high-density modern skyscrapers inhabited predominantly by compact nuclear households. As rural-to-urban migration accelerates, municipal authorities confront the formidable challenge of expanding municipal infrastructure while preventing the erosion of cultural kinship and social harmony that historically bound neighborhoods together.

[Paragraph B] A central dilemma in contemporary architectural planning revolves around whether to demolish aging structures or undertake extensive adaptive renovation. In numerous metropolises, historic industrial warehouses and decrepit Victorian tenements have been converted into eco-friendly residential lofts and community art centers. Architectural historians emphasize that preserving historic facades maintains civic continuity and psychological grounding for citizens, whereas reckless demolition often replaces irreplaceable cultural heritage with sterile, homogeneous concrete towers.

[Paragraph C] Parallel to architectural shifts, municipal transit networks have reached critical breaking points. Unregulated vehicular growth has generated severe traffic bottlenecks, exacerbating daily commuting fatigue and atmospheric emissions. In response, progressive municipal administrations are heavily subsidizing electrified light rail networks and designated pedestrian boulevards. Empirical transport studies demonstrate that when cities offer reliable, affordable, and punctual public transit, private car usage declines significantly, revitalizing street-level commerce and fostering spontaneous public interaction.

[Paragraph D] Simultaneously, the surge in international tourism has transformed ancient historical landmarks into bustling economic engines. While ecotourism and heritage travel generate indispensable municipal revenue, unchecked visitor influx frequently imperils fragile archaeological monuments. Without stringent visitor regulations, sensitive ancient stone ruins suffer irreversible structural wear, and indigenous traditions risk being trivialized into commercial spectacles for souvenir hunters. Consequently, responsible tourism models emphasize carrying-capacity limits and visitor education.

[Paragraph E] At the heart of urban resilience lies the work of archaeologists and historians. Through systematic excavation of subterranean strata, researchers uncover ancient artifacts that illuminate how bygone civilizations coped with climate disruptions and urban density. By curating these relics in accessible public archives and modern museum exhibitions, contemporary societies gain profound insights into their shared ancestors. Ultimately, a prosperous modern metropolis must honor its archaeological roots, ensuring that modern engineering harmonizes with historical preservation.

IELTS WRITING TASK 2 TOPIC PROMPT:

"In many developing cities, historic buildings and ancient neighborhoods are being demolished to construct modern residential skyscrapers and transport infrastructure. Do the advantages of this urban transformation outweigh the disadvantages?"

💡 Gợi ý viết: Sử dụng cấu trúc đoạn PEEL (Point - Explain - Evidence - Link), áp dụng ít nhất 3 từ vựng học thuật (ví dụ: demolish, renovate, infrastructure, heritage, historic) và cấu trúc so sánh hoặc Present Perfect Passive (*have been converted/demolished*).
🏆 BENCHMARK MODEL ESSAY (BAND 8.5+ ACADEMIC RIGOR)

On the one hand, replacing dilapidated urban quarters with modern high-density skyscrapers addresses critical housing shortages and enhances public transit infrastructure. In booming metropolises, constructing electrified metro lines and energy-efficient residential complexes substantially mitigates chronic traffic congestion and elevates living standards for working-class citizens. On the other hand, the irreversible demolition of ancient architectural landmarks severs a community's tangible link to its historical heritage. When historic neighborhoods are systematically leveled, cities lose their cultural uniqueness and risk becoming sterile concrete landscapes that alienate both inhabitants and heritage tourists. In my view, urban progress should not necessitate cultural destruction; municipal authorities ought to adopt adaptive renovation, ensuring that ancient facades are meticulously preserved while interiors are upgraded to satisfy modern technological demands.

✨ Academic Highlights: dilapidated urban quarters, mitigates chronic traffic congestion, severs a community's tangible link, sterile concrete landscapes, adaptive renovation, meticulously preserved.

"Do you prefer living in a modern apartment complex or a traditional detached family home?"

Although traditional houses offer expansive gardens, contemporary apartments provide round-the-clock security and require far less maintenance, which perfectly suits my busy academic lifestyle."
QUESTION 2 (PART 2 MINI)
"Describe an ancient museum exhibition or historical landmark that made a lasting impression on you."
Sample Band 8.5 Answer: "A truly unforgettable site I visited was the Imperial Citadel in Hue. The archaeological grounds feature centuries-old dynasties with meticulously preserved stone monuments and royal archives. Observing the ancient bronze artifacts firsthand gave me a profound appreciation for our ancestors' architectural craftsmanship."
QUESTION 3 (PART 3 STYLE)
"How can municipal governments encourage urban commuters to abandon private vehicles in favor of public transport?"
Sample Band 8.5 Answer: "In my view, authorities must adopt a dual strategy. On the one hand, they need to heavily subsidize railway fares and guarantee punctuality so that commuting by train is significantly more cost-effective than driving. On the other hand, implementing congestion charges in central business districts creates a powerful economic deterrent against private automobile usage."
